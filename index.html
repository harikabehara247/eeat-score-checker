Frequently asked questions

Is this Google's E-E-A-T score?

No. Google does not publish an E-E-A-T score. E-E-A-T comes from Google's Search Quality Rater Guidelines and describes what raters look for. This tool checks the visible, on-page signals that support it — author details, sources, dates, policies and first-hand experience — so you can find gaps quickly.

Why paste HTML instead of a URL?

Browsers block one website from reading another website's pages directly. Pasting the source keeps the tool free, private and server-less. Everything runs on your device.

Will a higher score improve my rankings?

Not by itself. These signals help readers and quality raters trust a page. Rankings depend on many other factors, including how well the content answers the search.

Which pages should I check first?

Pages about money, health, safety or legal topics ("Your Money or Your Life" pages) need the strongest trust signals. Start there, then check your highest-traffic articles.
